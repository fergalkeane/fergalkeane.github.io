01 / The Challenge

A trusted high-street institution with almost no presence online.

Since 1926 Daverns had built its name on personal service: fittings, styling advice and a carefully curated wall of designer labels on Main Street, Cashel. But that experience lived entirely in-store. Their small web presence couldn't carry the brand's quality, surface twenty-plus designer houses, or reassure a customer spending €345 on a coat they couldn't try on.

02 / The Solution

A heritage-led storefront that sells the way Daverns does in person.

The redesign treats the shop's founding mark (the crown, the 1926 date, the deep navy and gold) as the spine of the whole experience. Editorial brand storytelling, a designer-first discovery model and generous product photography recreate the curated, advisory feel of the store, while virtual appointments bring the styling service itself online.

03 / Impact

A full menswear & womenswear catalogue and 20+ luxury designer houses brought online, with virtual styling carried over from the shop floor.

02.3 / Brand Identity

A crown borrowed from the skyline

Daverns sits in the shadow of the Rock of Cashel, and the shop has traded under that skyline since 1926. The mark takes the rooftop silhouette of the Rock and reduces it to a small gold crown, so the town's most recognisable outline becomes the shop's emblem without a word of explanation.

Primary Lockup

Wordmark, crown and date

Wide-tracked capitals in navy carry the family name, the gold crown and the 1926 date sit above as a single rule-bound unit, and “of Cashel” anchors the place. Nothing moves independently, which keeps the mark stable on a shopfront, a swing tag or a 32px favicon.

Origin

The Rock of Cashel

The gables, the walls and the two towers of the Rock give the crown its five-peak rhythm. It is a local reference before it is a heraldic one.

Colourways

One mark, six grounds

The identity had to survive a seasonal shop. Navy and gold are the constants, and a working set of wine, tan, walnut and slate blue lets window cards, gift boxes and campaign pages change with the season while the mark itself never redraws. Every version is a single-weight knockout: no shadows, no outlines, no second lockup to police.

Positioning

Considered, not loud

The reference points are tailoring and neutral cloth rather than fast fashion, which set the tone for the photography and the pace of the site.

In Hand

The carrier bag test

Most people meet the brand on the street, holding it. The bag reverses to navy on white with the crown at full size, the strongest piece of local advertising the shop owns.

02.4 / Social

One line, four ways to say it

A shop this size cannot shoot its own campaigns every season, so the social system was built to make supplied designer imagery look like Daverns. “Masters of Luxury” stays fixed, the crown badge tucks into a corner, and the only decisions left each month are which photograph to use and which corner is quietest.

Fixed furnitureWide-tracked capitals, a chevron and a rule under “Shop Now”. Same size, same tracking, every post, so the feed reads as a run rather than four separate ads.
Two logo statesThe full lockup when the image is the brand's own, the navy D badge when the image belongs to a designer house. It never competes with a supplier's own mark.
Type takes the groundWhite on dark photography, navy on pale studio shots, gold on warm neutrals. Three states cover almost anything a supplier sends.
