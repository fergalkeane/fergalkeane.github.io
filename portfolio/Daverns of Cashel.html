I led UI/UX and brand design for Daverns of Cashel, from the visual identity through to the eCommerce site, packaging and social media.

Scope
Brand identity and visual direction, eCommerce UX and UI, website and digital experience, packaging and brand applications, and social media creative direction.
Client
A family-run luxury fashion and styling house on Main Street, Cashel, trading since 1926 and stocking twenty-plus designer labels.
Audience
Menswear and womenswear customers who know the shop for its personal service, and new customers meeting it online for the first time.
The Challenge

A century of personal service, brought online.

Since 1926 Daverns has built its name on personal service: fittings, styling advice and a carefully chosen range of designer labels on Main Street, Cashel. The challenge was to carry that experience onto the screen, presenting twenty-plus designer houses with the quality they have in store and giving a customer the confidence to buy a €345 coat they cannot try on first.

The Solution

A heritage-led storefront that sells the way Daverns does in person.

The crown, the 1926 date and the deep navy and gold run through the whole experience. Editorial brand storytelling, a designer-first way to browse and generous product photography carry the curated, advisory feel of the shop onto the screen, and the same identity extends to packaging and social media.

The Product

A guided walk through Daverns of Cashel

From the welcome storefront down to a single garment, every screen leads with the brand and the designer name first, then the product: the same order of trust a customer feels walking through the door on Main Street.

Storefront

The welcome, set in brand

The homepage leads with a styling-led welcome and ‘The Daverns Promise’, a founder’s note on luxury clothing and personal service, before any product appears. A first-time visitor meets the shop’s voice and values the way they would on Main Street, then steps into menswear or womenswear.

Departments

Two front doors, one language

Menswear and womenswear each open on a full-bleed editorial landing, then split into the same four routes: Clothing, Footwear, Accessories and Brands. Keeping the structure identical means a customer who learns one department already knows the other; only the imagery changes.

Designers

Shop the label, not the rack

Daverns customers come for the names: Gant, Mos Mosh, Farah, Basler, Joseph Ribkoff. So the designer directory is a primary route rather than a filter buried in a menu: a single wall of twenty-plus houses, each with a portrait-led tile, framed by the brand’s own ‘we have always broken the mould’ line.

Listing

Refine without losing the room

Within a brand or category, a three-up grid keeps the photography large. The ‘Refine search’ rail (brand, price, colour, category and size, each with live counts) stays quiet in navy and gold, so the clothing rather than the controls holds the customer’s eye.

Product

Everything you’d ask an assistant

In the shop, an assistant answers questions about fit, fabric and stock. The product page does the same on screen: a large gallery with alternate angles, stock status, colour and size selectors, a sizing guide, and description, fabric and delivery tabs. Previous and next products flank the image, so browsing never dead-ends.

Visual Identity

A crown borrowed from the skyline

Daverns sits in the shadow of the Rock of Cashel, and the shop has traded under that skyline since 1926. The mark takes the rooftop silhouette of the Rock and reduces it to a small gold crown, so the town’s most recognisable outline becomes the shop’s emblem without a word of explanation.

Origin

The Rock of Cashel

The gables, the walls and the two towers of the Rock give the crown its five-peak rhythm. It is a local reference before it is a heraldic one.

Primary Lockup

Wordmark, crown and date

Wide-tracked capitals in navy carry the family name, the gold crown and the 1926 date sit above as a single rule-bound unit, and ‘of Cashel’ anchors the place. Nothing moves independently, which keeps the mark stable on a shopfront, a swing tag or a 32px favicon.

Typography & Colour

Built around a 1926 mark

The crown emblem, the deep navy and a single restrained gold do the heavy lifting. They carry from the storefront hero to category filters and footer, so the whole shop feels like one tailored house rather than a template.

Display & Interface
Colourways

One mark, six grounds

The identity had to survive a seasonal shop. Navy and gold are the constants, and a working set of wine, tan, walnut and slate blue lets window cards, gift boxes and campaign pages change with the season while the mark itself never redraws. Every version is a single-weight knockout: no shadows, no outlines, no second lockup to police.

Social Media

One line, four ways to say it

Daverns sells other people’s labels, so most of its imagery arrives from the designer houses themselves. The social system gives that supplied photography a recognisable Daverns identity without competing with the labels: ‘Masters of Luxury’ stays fixed, the crown badge sits in a corner, and each post comes down to two decisions, which photograph and which corner is quietest. It is repeatable enough to run season after season and flexible enough to take whatever a supplier sends.

Fixed furnitureWide-tracked capitals, a chevron and a rule under ‘Shop Now’. Same size, same tracking, every post, so the feed reads as a run rather than four separate ads.
Two logo statesThe full lockup when the image is the brand’s own, the navy D badge when the image belongs to a designer house. It never competes with a supplier’s own mark.
Type takes the groundWhite on dark photography, navy on pale studio shots, gold on warm neutrals. Three states cover almost anything a supplier sends.
Outcome

One brand, from Main Street to the screen

The project delivered a complete brand and digital system for Daverns: a refined identity built around the crown, an eCommerce site covering menswear, womenswear and twenty-plus designer houses, packaging and swing tags for the shop, and a social media system for supplied imagery.

Together they let Daverns present itself online with the same care it shows in the shop, so customers meet one consistent brand whether they are on Main Street, carrying a bag home or browsing on a phone.
