I designed the brand identity and website for FitzGerald’s of Kells: the logo device and wordmark, the colour palette and visual direction, and the website with its project page template.

My scope
Brand identity, visual direction and website design, including the reusable layout for presenting completed commissions.
The client
A family cabinet-making, joinery and Corian fabrication business in Kells, Co. Meath. The furniture, interiors and photography of finished projects shown here are their work.
Audience
Architects, developers, specifiers and private clients deciding who to trust with a commission.
The Client

Sixty years of craft, presented like a supplier.

FitzGerald’s of Kells have been making furniture since 1963 and are one of Ireland’s leading cabinet makers and Corian fabricators, with award-winning work in both the Irish and international markets. Their reputation is built on precision and finish, and it travels almost entirely by word of mouth.

The problem was visibility, not quality. Anyone who had seen the workshop understood the standard immediately. Anyone meeting the brand for the first time, particularly an architect or specifier abroad, saw a trade supplier rather than a maker of considered, commissioned pieces.

The client brief

“Take 50 years of tradition and make it modern, and at the same time be respectful of our heritage, history and craftsmanship.”

The Approach

Precision as the visual argument.

The brief asked for two things that pull in opposite directions: modern and traditional. Rather than meet in the middle, I gave each half of the logo one job. The device signals precision and engineering; the typography carries the heritage.

The device comes from the FitzGerald coat of arms, simplified and redrawn on a grid so it reads as something made rather than drawn. Clean lines and exact spacing echo the joinery itself.

Trade-offs

Restraint, so the work is the loudest thing.

The most important decision was a limited colour palette. Furniture photography is full of warm timber, stone, white Corian and changing light, and any strong brand colour would compete with it.

The trade-off is an identity that can look quiet on its own but works in use. The brand recedes so the joinery does not have to fight it, which is the right priority for a maker whose portfolio does the selling.

Brand Principles

Tradition meets modern design

The mark developed in three steps: the original coat of arms, a simplified shield, and finally an interlocking FG monogram re-cut from the saltire, with the crown and laurel removed.

Three rules the identity had to obey

01

Heritage, not nostalgia

The coat of arms earns its place because the family and the workshop are genuinely continuous. It is reduced to a mark, never treated as an antique.

02

Precision is the promise

Spacing, alignment and construction are visibly exact. A brand for makers of millimetre-tolerance joinery cannot be loosely set.

03

The work comes first

Limited colour, generous space, minimal ornament. Every layout decision defers to the photography of the finished pieces.

The Website

A portfolio that behaves like a showroom

The site has one job: convince an architect, developer or private client that this workshop can execute their commission. Structure follows how that decision is actually made, by looking at completed work in detail.

Home Page

Open on the work, state the credentials underneath

The homepage leads with full-width workshop photography, then a short welcome, a grid of completed projects, awards, the team and clients. Navigation stays at four items (Home, Our Work, Our Team, Contact Us), so the projects are never more than one click away.

fitzgeraldsofkells.ie

Case Study Pages

One template, every commission tells the same story

Every project page uses the same structure: a full-width photograph with the project name, a written description, a Project Info panel with the client and team, any awards, and a gallery of the finished work. New commissions are added in the same format, so every project reads with the same authority.

Outcome

A stronger identity. A digital showroom worthy of the work.

The identity gives FitzGerald’s a mark that reflects both the precision of its joinery and the continuity of a family business established in 1963. The website presents completed commissions the way an architect or specifier evaluates them: the finished space first, then the brief, the project team and any awards, with the workshop’s cabinet-making and Corian capabilities introduced on the homepage.
