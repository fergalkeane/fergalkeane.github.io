01 / The Problem

A training portal that looked like a homepage.

Training material was spread across formats and organised like a corporate site, with category tiles and news up front. A technician arriving with a required course due had to work to find it.

The people using it are A&P technicians, leads and inspectors. They think in aircraft types, ATA chapters and task cards, and they often fit training in between jobs.

02 / Framing

Design it as a training tool, not an intranet.

The redesign starts from the technician's question: what do I need to do next? Home opens on the course in progress and what is due, with browsing one step away rather than the first thing on the page.

Content is indexed the way maintenance is: by aircraft and ATA chapter, as well as by learning area. A search for “A320 29” goes straight to the right course.

03 / The Hard Constraint

Four formats, one way to learn.

Training material arrives as video, PDF task cards and references, slide decks and knowledge checks. Instead of sending each to a different viewer, every lesson plays inside a single player with the same header, progress bar and Previous / Next controls.

The same structure has to hold on a desktop at home and on a phone in the hangar, so navigation collapses to a slim rail on desktop and a top bar on mobile.

04 / Outcome

Required training front and centre, with every format in one consistent player.

02.1 / Design Principles

Three rules for training between jobs

01

Lead with what's due

The course in progress and assigned training come first, with due dates shown as dates, not buried in a list.

02

Speak maintenance

Aircraft codes and ATA chapters sit alongside titles everywhere, and both are searchable and filterable.

03

One player, every format

Video, PDF, slides and quizzes share the same frame and controls, so the format never changes how a lesson works.

02.3 / White Label

Built for Delta, ready for other airlines

Delta owns the portal, and the plan is to license it to other carriers' maintenance organisations. Brand lives in a small set of tokens: logo, two core colours and an accent. Swapping them re-skins the whole product without touching layouts, components or content structure. Here is the same home screen as a Lufthansa deployment.

lufthansa / home

01

Tokens, not themes

Primary, surface and accent colours plus the logo are the only brand inputs. Everything else derives from them.

02

Contrast holds

Accent-on-text pairs are checked per brand, so a yellow accent gets dark button text where Delta's red uses white.

03

Content stays generic

Courses are keyed by aircraft type and ATA chapter, which every operator shares, so a new airline can load its own library into the same structure.
